See inside a robot's software

A ROS 2 robot is a team of small programs called nodes. They pass messages on named topics: the camera publishes images, the brain decides, the wheels obey. Tap any node or topic in the graph to see the real message flowing through it.

By Week 6 you write nodes like these yourself, in Python and in C++.

Three ways ROS 2 nodes talk

Most ROS 2 programs are built from just these three patterns. Here they are, slowed down.

Topic: a radio station

A publisher broadcasts on a named channel such as /scan. Any node tuned to that exact name hears every message. The lidar doesn't know, or care, who is listening.

Used for streams: sensor data, camera images, speed commands.

Service: ordering at a counter

A client asks one question and waits for one answer, like "spawn a turtle at (2, 2)" or "what is the battery level?". Quick in, quick out.

Used for short jobs that finish right away.

Action: a pizza you track on an app

A client sends a goal, gets feedback while the job runs ("in the oven", "on the way"), and finally a result. It can cancel at any time.

Used for long jobs: navigating to a room, moving a robot arm.

Concept lab: poke the ideas

Short animations that explain one idea at a time and narrate every step. Press the buttons, break things on purpose, and read what happens. The same animations appear inside the lessons.

    Questions students ask

    Is it really free?

    Yes. Everyone who registers by 31 December 2026 joins the founding batch for free.

    Do I need Ubuntu or a powerful laptop?

    Not to start. Days 1 to 9 need only a browser, on a laptop or a phone. On Day 10 you install ROS 2 Jazzy on Ubuntu 24.04 (WSL2, dual boot, a virtual machine or a lab computer). After that, every lesson up to Day 30 still works in the browser's practice terminal and Python playground, and you can repeat each step on your own Ubuntu. Ubuntu helps most for the final project, but it is not required. Weeks 7 to 9 (URDF and RViz) also work in the browser, with a practice 3D RViz.

    Can I learn on my phone?

    Yes. Lessons, animations, games and tests work on a phone. A laptop is more comfortable for typing code in the practice terminal.

    How long can I use the course?

    Exactly 60 days from the day your registration is approved. If you finish every lesson of Weeks 1 to 6 and score at least 60% in each of their tests within those 60 days, your access stays open for life so you can revise later. In your first registration you can ask once for up to 30 extra days. If the course is not finished in time, the registration is cancelled; you can then ask to register again, afresh, at most two times.

    How do the weekly tests work?

    Each week ends with a timed test that unlocks when you finish that week's lessons. You get two attempts and your best score counts; you need 60% to pass. Every student gets a different paper.

    What is the personal project?

    After the last lesson you get your own small ROS 2 project, such as a turtle that patrols waypoints or a safety filter, with names and numbers chosen just for you. You submit your code and a short report in Python or C++, up to two times. Your instructor marks it; 80% passes.

    What does the certificate show, and can employers check it?

    Your name and details, the modules completed, every test mark, your project score, the issue date and a 2-year validity, signed by the instructor. Its QR code and ID open a page on ros2lab.com that confirms it is genuine.

    Python or C++?

    Both. From Week 5, every idea comes first in Python and then in C++, so you can follow either or both.

    Who can join?

    Students (UG, PG or PhD), graduates, faculty, research scholars and engineers. If you are under 18, register with a parent's or guardian's consent.
